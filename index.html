AI Reality Breaker System 💥 An index that knows where every idea in the corpus belongs, and keeps that separate from where the file happens to live.

This system organizes a research corpus of 508 records — long-form theory notes, syntheses and readings. The problem it solves is ordinary and awkward: most records genuinely belong under several subjects at once, so a single folder tree either duplicates them or loses them. The AI Reality Breaker System answers that by splitting the job in two. Files stay in the four stores they were written into. A separate read-only routing layer — one master index, 36 main hubs, 120 sub-hubs — says where each record belongs, and is free to file the same record in as many places as it honestly fits. Right now 83% of records are filed in more than one place.

How it works

Four moving parts. Read them in order and the five diagrams below stop needing explanation.

ENTRY

One master index

A single note is the only way in. It holds no theory of its own — only the map. Its whole job is to hand you off to the right hub.

TIER 1

36 main hubs

The top-level subjects, T01–T36. Each hub is a view across the entire corpus, not a folder: it collects records from all four stores without moving any of them.

TIER 2

120 sub-hubs

Each hub splits into three or four sub-hubs — the actual shelves. A sub-hub is the smallest unit that holds records, and it is where search actually lands.

STORAGE

Four stores

Where files physically live: A, M, O, C. A record has exactly one store and any number of shelves. The routing layer never writes to a store, which is why it can be rebuilt from scratch at any time.

The four stores

The store is a physical fact — which corpus the file was written into. It is deliberately not the same thing as the subject, and the routing layer is the component that refuses to reconcile the two.

A

CLAUDE ARCHITECTURE

cold store
142records · 28% of corpus

Normalized, substrate-independent law. The settled version of a mechanism, written so it does not depend on the domain it came from. Read often, rewritten rarely.

M

CLAUDE MAIN

hot store
242records · 48% of corpus

The working corpus. Domain-loaded, write-heavy, and the place new theory lands first. Largest store by a wide margin, and the one that changes most.

O

CLAUDE OUTPUTS

serving layer
62records · 12% of corpus

Finished answers assembled from the other stores — the equivalent of a materialized view. Built to be read straight through, not edited.

C

CREATIVE OUTPUT

serving layer
62records · 12% of corpus

Renderings, readings and informal logs — the same material in a looser register. Also read-optimized, also derived.

The lanes run the way a database would: M takes the writes, compaction normalizes finished material down into A, and O and C serve derived reads built from both. Splitting hot from cold is what keeps a 142-record store of settled law stable while a 242-record working corpus churns beside it.

Figure 3

Partitions and lanes

Where records physically live, how far each store spreads across the index, and why the two partitions are never merged.

Left panel is the partition table: each store's record count, its share of the corpus, how many hubs it reaches, and how much of it is cross-routed. Right is the lane diagram — A cold feeding M hot, M compacting back into A, and both serving O and C — with the router drawn off to the side because it writes into none of them.

That last detail is the important one. The router is a read layer. It can be deleted and rebuilt from the stores without losing a single record.

Hub load

The 36 hubs are a second storage system: the stores say where a record sits on disk, the hubs say where it sits in the argument. That shelf is uneven on purpose.

The 508 records occupy 879 hub slots, because a record filed under three subjects takes a slot in each. The heaviest hub carries 39 records and the lightest 9. Nothing was padded to even the shelf out — the distribution is a readout of what the corpus is actually about.
